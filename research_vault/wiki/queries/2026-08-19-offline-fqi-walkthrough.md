# Offline FQI — A Walkthrough

A study thread working from the FQI template down to one concrete offline algorithm. Reference material lives on the [[fitted-q-iteration]], [[extrapolation-error]] and [[pessimistic-fitted-q-learning]] pages; this page is the *path through* them, keeping the questions in the order they actually arose.

## 1. What is FQI, and what is being fitted?

RL becomes repeated supervised learning on a fixed batch. Given $\mathcal{D} = \{(s_i,a_i,r_i,s_i')\}$ and a current $Q_{k-1}$, form targets $y_i = r_i + \gamma\max_{a'}Q_{k-1}(s_i',a')$, fit $Q_k$ to $\{((s_i,a_i), y_i)\}$ by least squares, repeat.

The clarifying question is *what* is being fitted. Not the dynamics $P(s'|s,a)$, and not a reward model — the $Q$-function directly, with **labels generated by the previous iterate**. Each round is an ordinary regression whose training signal is a moving target computed from the model's own last guess. That is simultaneously why FQI is simple, why it is model-free while still performing dynamic programming, and why it can be unstable.

Each sweep propagates value backward exactly one step, which is why $K$ iterations buy $K$ steps of lookahead. Worked example and the $\Pi_\mathcal{F}\mathcal{T}$ view: [[fitted-q-iteration]].

## 2. Does the algorithm depend on offline vs. online?

**No — and this is the cleanest way to hold the two ideas apart:**

> FQI determines how you learn from data. Offline vs. online determines how you obtain it.

The update inspects no provenance. It cannot tell whether a transition was logged last year by a human, produced by an unrelated policy, or generated moments ago by the current one. What changes is whether $\mathcal{D}$ can grow in response to what has been learned — fixed forever offline, versus $\mathcal{D}_1 \to Q_1 \to \text{collect with } \pi_1 \to \mathcal{D}_2 \to \cdots$ online.

The consequence is usually stated as a difficulty (online data is non-stationary and learner-dependent), but for the failure mode that dominates offline RL it is a *gift*: an erroneous $Q(s,B) = 100$ is **self-refuting** online, since acting on it generates the evidence that kills it, and **self-reinforcing** offline. Full comparison table in the *Offline vs. online FQI* section of [[fitted-q-iteration]].

So pessimism, conservatism, and support constraints are not a rival theory of Bellman regression. They are compensation for a missing feedback loop.

## 3. The multi-action question

A worked example with a single action $A$ hides the difficulty, because $\max_{a'}$ is then trivial. With $\mathcal{A} = \{A,B,C\}$ and a dataset containing only $(s_3,A,10,\text{terminal})$, the iteration-2 target is

$$
y_2(s_2,A) = 0 + 0.9\max_{a'\in\{A,B,C\}} Q_1(s_3,a'),
$$

and the regression loss never constrained $Q_1(s_3,B)$ or $Q_1(s_3,C)$ at all.

**Can the fitted model still evaluate them?** Yes. $Q_\theta$ is a *function*, not a table, with parameters shared across inputs — the linear case makes it explicit, $Q_\theta(s_3,B) = \phi(s_3,B)^\top\theta$ with $\theta$ estimated from entirely different examples. A network over discrete actions emits $|\mathcal{A}|$ numbers from one forward pass regardless of what was observed.

**Should we trust them?** Different question entirely, and the one the whole subfield is organized around. Keep apart:

- *Can* it produce a value off-support? **Yes** — a function approximator is defined off its training points.
- *Is* that value accurate? **Not necessarily.**

## 4. Why this is worse than ordinary estimation error

Because $\max$ is not an average. Even with zero-mean error, the maximization **preferentially selects whichever unsupported action was overvalued** — a selection effect layered on top of any bias. And it compounds: the contaminated target feeds the next regression, which feeds the next.

Worth separating from [[overestimation-bias]], which is the noise-driven cousin present even when every action is observed. Extrapolation error is a *coverage* failure that no amount of data on the wrong distribution repairs. Three coverage cases, the unseen-pair vs. unseen-action distinction, and the tabular contrast: [[extrapolation-error]].

This also explains why concentrability and [[coverage-coefficient]]-style conditions are unavoidable rather than technical conveniences — FQI's greedy $\pi_k$ induces $d^{\pi_k}$, which need not resemble $d^\mu$.

## 5. Two ways to fix it

| | Mechanism | Vault page |
|---|---|---|
| Penalize the query | subtract an uncertainty width from $\widehat{Q}$ | [[pessimism-principle]], [[pessimistic-fitted-q-learning]] |
| Refuse the query | restrict the max to in-sample actions, smoothed as an upper expectile | [[implicit-q-learning]] |

Worth noting that vanilla FQI genuinely maximizes over all of $\mathcal{A}$, *not* over dataset-supported actions. Those are different algorithms — and the second one is essentially what IQL implements.

## 6. Algorithm 1 of [[Yin2023Offline]] as the concrete case

PFQL is the FQI template with pessimism inserted **inside the backup**. Lines 4 and 9 of Algorithm 1 alone are plain FQI; lines 5–8 are the entire modification: build the gradient Gram matrix $\Sigma_h$, form the width $\Gamma_h = \beta/\sqrt{m(s,a)}$ where $m(s,a)$ is the effective sample size along $\nabla_\theta f$, subtract it, clip.

The placement is the design. $\hat V_h$ becomes the *next* iteration's target, so the value being backed up already has $\Gamma_h$ subtracted — an unsupported action gets a large penalty and loses the max before it can contaminate anything downstream. Penalizing only at policy extraction would give a cautious policy computed from values that were already poisoned.

Line-by-line reading, the finite-horizon indexing, the hyperparameter conditions, the linear/tabular/GLM specializations, and the circularity in $\Gamma_h$ (it is built from the very $\hat\theta_h$ it protects against): [[pfql-algorithm-1]]. Concept-level treatment: [[pessimistic-fitted-q-learning]].

## Where this leaves the open questions

- The suboptimality decomposition charges $\sum_h 2\mathbb{E}_{\pi^*}[\Gamma_h]$ — the penalty along the *comparator's* trajectory. That is what makes the bound instance-dependent, and connects this thread to [[instance-dependent-bounds]].
- Both fixes are stated against coverage conditions that are binary or all-policy. A density-aware replacement is the live gap — see [[coverage-coefficient]] and the open problems on [[offline-reinforcement-learning]].
- Nobody has checked whether these instance functionals predict observed difficulty on a real benchmark.
