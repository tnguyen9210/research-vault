# Foster2025Foundation — Sections 1–4 Summary

## Section 1 — Setup and Motivation

**The problem.** Online LM alignment needs *active exploration* (deliberately generating diverse responses) to achieve superhuman capabilities. Popular methods (PPO, OnlineDPO) use *passive* exploration — they just sample from the current policy and hope to stumble on good responses. Active exploration algorithms exist in theory but are computationally intractable for LMs because they require enumerating the exponentially large response space $\mathcal{Y}$.

**The framework.** The paper abstracts the LM as a *sampling oracle* and studies two settings:
- **Weak oracle:** can sample $y \sim \pi_\text{ref}(\cdot|x)$ — just the base model
- **Strong oracle:** can sample $y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)$ for any $\theta \in \Theta$

Complexity is measured by two quantities: $T_\text{data}$ (reward oracle calls — expensive, e.g. human feedback or formal verification) and $T_\text{comp}$ (sampling oracle calls — cheap, just running the LM).

**Policy class.** Linear softmax: $\pi_\theta(y|x) \propto \pi_\text{ref}(y|x) \cdot \exp(\beta^{-1}\langle\theta, \phi(x,y)\rangle)$. This is the natural RLHF parameterization — it's a DPO-trained model where only the last layer changes. The objective is KL-regularized reward $J_\beta(\pi) = J(\pi) - \beta \cdot D_\text{KL}(\pi \|\pi_\text{ref})$.

**The tension.** XPO (Xie et al. 2024) achieves good $T_\text{data}$ but its bonus term introduces non-convexity — unknown whether minimizable in polynomial time. The paper asks: *can we get both good $T_\text{data}$ and polynomial $T_\text{comp}$?*

## Section 2 — Coverage is Computationally Necessary

**The coverage coefficient.**
$$C_\text{cov}(\pi^*_\beta) := \sup_{x,y} \frac{\pi^*_\beta(y|x)}{\pi_\text{ref}(y|x)}$$
This measures how much the optimal policy deviates from the base model. Large $C_\text{cov}$ means $\pi_\text{ref}$ puts low mass on the best responses — they are "hidden" from the base model. Always $C_\text{cov} \leq \exp(R_\text{max}/\beta)$.

**Theorem 2.1 (lower bound).** For *any* algorithm in the sampling oracle framework:
$$T_\text{comp}(\varepsilon, \delta) \geq \Omega\!\left(\min\!\left\{e^{\beta^2 d/2},\, e^{\beta^{-1}/2},\, C_\text{cov}(\pi^*_\beta)\right\}\right)$$
unless the algorithm is also data-inefficient ($T_\text{data} \geq |\mathcal{Y}|/8$).

**Intuition.** There's a single "hidden" optimal response $y^*$ that gets low mass under $\pi_\text{ref}$. To find it, any algorithm must either (i) brute-force search over $\theta$ (exponential in $d$) or (ii) draw enough samples from $\pi_\text{ref}$ to observe $y^*$ by chance (costs $\approx C_\text{cov}$). There's no free lunch.

**Key implication.** Coverage is *not* necessary for data efficiency ($T_\text{data}$) — XPO achieves $T_\text{data} = \tilde{O}(d^2/\varepsilon^2)$ regardless of $C_\text{cov}$. But it *is* necessary for compute efficiency ($T_\text{comp}$). This means coverage is a "hidden knowledge" problem: if the base model already knows roughly where good responses are, exploration is cheap; otherwise you're stuck.

## Section 3 — SpannerSampling: Achieving the Lower Bound

SpannerSampling achieves $T_\text{comp} = \tilde{O}(C_\text{cov})$ per round using only the *weak* oracle — matching the Thm 2.1 lower bound.

**Phase 1 — Spanner construction.** Sample pairs $(y_1, y_2) \sim \pi_\text{ref}(\cdot|x)^{\otimes 2}$ until you find pairs whose *relative feature* $\varphi(x,y_1,y_2) = \phi(x,y_1) - \phi(x,y_2)$ is "informative" — i.e., points in a direction not yet covered by the second-moment matrix $\Sigma_\text{span}$. These pairs form the *spanner* $\Psi_\text{span}$, a compact set of size $\text{poly}(d)$ that covers the feature space in directions that matter for $\pi^*_\beta$.

Crucially: the number of *reward* queries needed to build the spanner is $\text{poly}(d)$, independent of $C_\text{cov}$. You might need many oracle samples to *find* each spanner pair (costing $\sim C_\text{cov}$ per pair), but the spanner itself stays small.

**Phase 2 — Exploration.** Fit $\theta^t$ via least squares on accumulated data. Then generate the next response using a *truncated* softmax policy:
$$\bar\pi_{\theta^t}(y|x) \propto \pi_\text{ref}(y|x) \cdot \exp(\beta^{-1}\langle\theta^t, \varphi(x,y,y')\rangle) \cdot \mathbf{1}[\|\varphi(x,y,y')\|_{\Sigma^{-1}_\text{span}} \leq \nu]$$
The indicator truncates directions already covered by the spanner. This makes the density ratio $\bar\pi_{\theta^t}/\pi_\text{ref}$ bounded by $C_\text{cov}(\pi^*_\beta)$ (not $C_\text{cov}(\pi_{\theta^t})$, which could be much larger). So rejection sampling from $\pi_\text{ref}$ costs only $\tilde{O}(C_\text{cov}(\pi^*_\beta))$ per sample.

**Theorem 3.1 (guarantee).**
$$T_\text{data} = \tilde{O}\!\left(\frac{d^2}{\min\{\varepsilon,\beta\}}\right), \qquad T_\text{comp} = \tilde{O}\!\left(C_\text{cov}(\pi^*_\beta) \cdot \frac{R_\text{max}^2}{\beta^2}\right) \cdot T_\text{data}^2$$

Note $T_\text{data}$ achieves a *fast* $1/\varepsilon^2$ rate (vs. $1/\varepsilon$ for XPO) — the KL regularization induces strong convexity, which is a secondary benefit.

**The key insight.** SpannerSampling is *improper*: it never samples from $\pi_\theta \in \Pi$ to gather reward signal. It uses $\pi_\text{ref}$ for both phases, then applies rejection sampling at inference time. This improperness is what allows it to escape the hardness result in Section 4.

## Section 4 — Training-Time Interventions Cannot Be Efficient

**Definition (proper algorithm).** An algorithm is *proper* if at each step it queries the reward oracle with $y \sim \pi_{\theta^t}(\cdot|x)$ for some $\theta^t \in \Theta$ from the policy class. This captures any algorithm that trains a policy (DPO, XPO, PPO, REBEL, …) and uses that trained policy to collect data.

**Theorem 4.1.** Under ETH (Randomized Exponential Time Hypothesis), *no proper algorithm* can be simultaneously:
- Data-efficient: $T_\text{data} \leq \text{poly}(d, \beta^{-1}, \varepsilon^{-1}, \delta^{-1})$
- Compute-efficient: $T_\text{comp} \leq \text{poly}(d, \exp(\beta^{-1}), \varepsilon^{-1}, \delta^{-1})$

This applies to OnlineDPO, XPO, and any algorithm that computes $\pi^t = \arg\min_{\pi \in \Pi} L^t_\mathcal{D}(\pi)$ — regardless of how clever the loss $L^t$ is.

**Proof sketch.** Reduce from Max-$k$-DNF (finding a satisfying assignment for a $k$-DNF formula). Embed the formula into $\phi(x,y)$ so that clauses correspond to responses. Any proper algorithm must put non-negligible mass on the "null" response $y_0 = \mathbf{0}$ unless $\theta$ approximately satisfies a large fraction of clauses — but finding such $\theta$ is ETH-hard. SpannerSampling escapes this because it samples from $\pi_\text{ref}$ directly (improper), not from $\pi_{\theta^t}$.

**What this means practically.** The separation is sharp: SpannerSampling achieves what Thm 4.1 says proper algorithms cannot. The "training-time vs. inference-time" distinction is not just a practical engineering choice — it's a fundamental computational boundary.
